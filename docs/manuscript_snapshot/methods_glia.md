# Methods

## Study design and analytical framework

This exploratory secondary analysis used publicly available transcriptomic data. No new animal experiments were performed. The primary cohort was GSE267933 (Suo et al., 2025), which comprised six 18-month-old male C57BL/6 mice. The source report stated that mice were randomly assigned to the two groups but did not describe the allocation procedure. Three mice received 2.5% sevoflurane in 50% oxygen for 30 min plus exploratory laparotomy. Three control mice received 50% oxygen for 30 min without anesthesia or laparotomy. Hippocampi were collected 24 h later and processed for single-cell RNA sequencing with the 10x Genomics Chromium Single Cell 3′ v2 platform. Each mouse was represented by one sequencing library and constituted one biological replicate. Cells from the same mouse were not treated as independent biological replicates. Analyses compared the combined anesthesia-plus-laparotomy group with the oxygen-control group. Because there was no anesthesia-only group, the study could not determine whether between-group differences were attributable to anesthesia, laparotomy, or their interaction.

Two additional accessions were used for processing sensitivity and external context. GSE289098 (Zheng et al., 2025) contains an alternative processed-count matrix derived from the same six libraries as GSE267933. It was used only to assess sensitivity to count-matrix construction and did not provide additional biological replication. GSE283401 (Yin et al., 2024) contains bulk RNA sequencing of isolated hippocampal microglia from male mice. Formal analyses used the old-mouse subset. Exposed mice received 1.2% isoflurane in 30% oxygen balanced with nitrogen for 2 h plus laparotomy. Controls received the carrier gas for 2 h and the same bupivacaine and meloxicam regimen, but no anesthesia or laparotomy. Mice were sampled 6 or 48 h after the start of anesthesia or carrier-gas administration. The cohorts differed in age, anesthetic, exposure duration, assay, laboratory, and sampling design. GSE283401 therefore provided external cross-experiment context rather than replication or validation.

## Primary single-cell processing and analysis populations

The six GSE267933 feature–barcode matrices were processed in Scanpy (Wolf et al., 2018), with animal and exposure labels retained for each cell. Each matrix contained integer unique molecular identifier (UMI) counts. Cells expressing fewer than 200 or more than 5,000 genes, or with mitochondrial UMIs accounting for at least 20% of total counts, were excluded. Genes detected in fewer than three cells were also excluded. The resulting whole-cell dataset contained 18,114 cells and 18,936 genes. The integer UMI matrix was retained for pseudobulk and direct-expression analyses. For visualization and specified cell-level summaries, counts were normalized to 10,000 per cell and transformed as the natural logarithm of one plus the normalized count. Principal-component analysis was used for dimensionality reduction. A nearest-neighbor graph was then constructed, and unsupervised Leiden clustering (Traag et al., 2019) was used to group transcriptionally similar cells. Four clusters expressing canonical microglial markers, including *P2ry12*, *Tmem119*, *Cx3cr1*, and *Csf1r*, were annotated as microglia. These clusters contained 7,461 cells and formed the fixed reference microglial population.

The fixed microglial population was divided into seven numeric reference partitions for the partition audit described below. Three cell populations were then defined for distinct analyses. All 7,461 cells were included in partition and annotation assessments. Composition analyses included 7,367 cells after excluding numeric partition 6. Molecular analyses included 7,371 cells not flagged as predicted doublets by Scrublet (Wolock et al., 2019), irrespective of reference-partition identity. Scrublet was applied separately to each of the six libraries, and its calls were treated as model-based predictions rather than confirmed doublets. The complete 7,461-cell population was also examined in cell-inclusion sensitivity analyses. Thus, reference-partition identity determined the composition population but did not determine inclusion in the primary molecular analyses.

Unfiltered droplet matrices were unavailable for GSE267933. Ambient RNA could therefore not be estimated from empty droplets, and no ambient-RNA correction was applied. As a descriptive contamination assessment, raw-count detection of microglial, border-associated macrophage, choroid-plexus, and oligodendrocyte-lineage transcripts was examined across all seven reference partitions. This assessment was not used to alter cell inclusion, calculate a purity score, or support an inference of cellular purity. The complete transcript lists and assessment procedures are provided in the Supplementary Methods.

## Reference partition audit and animal-level composition

The fixed microglial population was represented by a seven-partition solution generated from principal components inherited from the whole-cell analysis. Leiden clustering was performed at resolution 0.4. Numeric identifiers 0–6 were used for all partition-level analyses. The accompanying biological names were retained only for traceability because they were not generated by a prespecified reproducible annotation rule. They were therefore not treated as validated microglial states. Partition markers were assessed descriptively by comparing each partition with all remaining microglia using one-versus-rest Wilcoxon tests. Benjamini–Hochberg adjustment (Benjamini and Hochberg, 1995) was applied within each partition. These cell-level marker statistics were used for annotation assessment and visualization, not for between-group inference.

Partition sensitivity was assessed using two complementary procedures. First, Leiden clustering was repeated on the stored nearest-neighbor graph while varying the random seed. Agreement with the reference partition was quantified using the adjusted Rand index. Second, neighborhood graphs were reconstructed after repeated 80% cell subsampling. The best correspondence between each reference partition and the reconstructed partitions was quantified using the Jaccard index. Additional analyses examined the number and recovery of partitions across a range of Leiden resolutions. These procedures resampled cells or varied computational settings, but did not resample animals or repeat the complete preprocessing pipeline. They therefore assessed conditional computational sensitivity rather than biological reproducibility or the existence of discrete biological states. Complete settings and summary definitions are provided in the Supplementary Methods.

Animal-level composition was evaluated using the six non-Rare reference partitions. For each mouse, the percentage of each partition was calculated relative to that mouse’s total number of non-Rare microglia. All six animal-level percentages were displayed. Between-group differences were summarized as the unweighted difference in mean percentage points, with a Welch–Satterthwaite 95% confidence interval and Cohen’s *d*. A two-sided exact permutation test evaluated all 20 possible assignments of three of the six mice to the combined anesthesia-plus-laparotomy group. These analyses were descriptive because the partition percentages were interdependent and the reference partition was computationally sensitive. They were not used to establish reproducible expansion or depletion of a microglial state.

## Animal-level transcriptomic analyses

Untransformed UMI counts from the 7,371 Scrublet-negative microglia were summed within each mouse, producing six pseudobulk expression profiles. Features with fewer than 10 total counts across the six profiles were excluded, leaving 13,926 features. Transcriptome-wide differential expression was analyzed with DESeq2 (Love et al., 2014). The model included exposure group as the sole explanatory variable and used the oxygen-control group as the reference. The estimated coefficient represented combined anesthesia-plus-laparotomy exposure relative to oxygen control. For each feature, DESeq2 estimated the unshrunk log2 fold change, its standard error, and a Wald-test *P* value. The corresponding 95% confidence interval was calculated as the estimated log2 fold change ± 1.96 standard errors. Benjamini–Hochberg adjustment was applied across features. To assess the influence of individual animals, the model was refitted six times, with one mouse omitted in each refit. These omissions were treated as influence diagnostics. No mouse was excluded from the full-cohort analysis.

Pathway-level associations were examined using all 50 gene sets in the mouse MSigDB 2026.1.Mm Hallmark collection (Liberzon et al., 2015; Castanza et al., 2023). Genes were ranked by the signed Wald statistic from the animal-level DESeq2 model. Positive values indicated ranking toward the combined anesthesia-plus-laparotomy group. Preranked gene-set enrichment analysis (Subramanian et al., 2005) was performed with clusterProfiler (Wu et al., 2021) for every Hallmark set. Benjamini–Hochberg adjustment was applied across all 50 results. Interferon alpha response, interferon gamma response, TNFA signaling via NF-κB, and inflammatory response were selected for focused presentation, while complete results were reported for all sets. The procedure was repeated after omitting each mouse in turn while retaining the feature universe defined in the full-cohort analysis. These refits assessed animal influence and were not treated as independent tests. Detailed identifier mapping, duplicate-symbol handling, and software settings are provided in the Supplementary Methods.

A secondary descriptive analysis summarized seven interferon-responsive transcripts selected during exploratory inspection of the primary dataset: *Irf7*, *Ifitm3*, *Isg15*, *Mx1*, *Ifit1*, *Ifit2*, and *Ifit3*. The panel was used to summarize animal-level expression patterns. For transcript *g* in mouse *j*, expression was calculated as `log2[((Y_jg + 0.5)/(N_j + 1)) × 10^6]`, where `Y_jg` was the transcript’s summed UMI count and `N_j` was the summed UMI count across all features in the retained microglia from that mouse. The animal-level panel value was the unweighted mean of the seven transcript values, so each transcript contributed equally. All six animal values, the between-group mean difference, a Welch–Satterthwaite 95% confidence interval, Cohen’s *d*, and six one-animal-omission estimates were reported. A two-sided exact permutation *P* value was calculated from all 20 possible assignments of three mice to each group and was reported descriptively. Detailed calculations and cell-inclusion sensitivity analyses are provided in the Supplementary Methods.

## Alternative estimator and same-cohort sensitivity analyses

To assess whether the seven-transcript pattern depended on how expression was summarized, background-adjusted module scores were calculated using `scanpy.tl.score_genes`. This method subtracts the mean expression of algorithmically matched control genes from the mean expression of the selected transcripts. It therefore measures selected-transcript expression relative to a computational reference, whereas the primary measure does not subtract a control-gene set. The symbol-deduplicated expression matrix was normalized to 10,000 counts per cell and log1p-transformed. Scoring used the same 7,371 retained cells without conditioning on the submitted partition. Scores were calculated using a fixed configuration of seed 42, `ctrl_size = 50`, and `n_bins = 25`, and were then averaged within each mouse. The full-cohort comparison and systematic one-mouse omissions were calculated from these six mouse-level scores. Matched control genes served only as an algorithmic reference and were not interpreted as a biological program. Direct transcript expression, background-adjusted module scores, and Hallmark enrichment were analyzed as separate measures. Complete parameter sweeps, matched-control decomposition, and transcript-omission analyses are provided in the Supplementary Methods.

An alternative processed-count matrix deposited as GSE289098 was derived from the same six source libraries as GSE267933. We used this matrix to assess whether the animal-level results were sensitive to count-matrix construction. The same 7,371 cell barcodes and their existing mouse and group labels were transferred by sample-aware one-to-one matching. No GSE289098-specific quality-control thresholds, microglial reclassification, clustering, or partition selection were applied. Counts were summed within each mouse, and the seven transcript-level expression values and equal-weight panel value were recalculated using the definitions applied to the primary matrix. The full-cohort comparison and six systematic one-mouse omissions were then repeated. A separate feature-level DESeq2 comparison using the common 13,926-feature universe is described in the Supplementary Methods. Both accessions represent alternative processing of the same cells and libraries. Matrix differences were therefore treated descriptively and were not assigned paired-test *P* values. This analysis assessed sensitivity to processed-count construction and did not provide additional biological replication or orthogonal validation.

## External contextual analysis using GSE283401

GSE283401 was used to provide external cross-experiment context from bulk RNA sequencing of isolated hippocampal microglia. Only the old-animal libraries were included. The deposited 6-h and 48-h count matrices were analyzed separately. The 6-h analysis included eight source-labeled controls and seven animals exposed to isoflurane anesthesia plus laparotomy. The 48-h analysis included eight source-labeled controls and nine exposed animals. At each time, transcript counts were analyzed with a separate DESeq2 model containing treatment group as the explanatory variable and control as the reference. Positive log2 fold changes indicated higher expression in the isoflurane-anesthesia-plus-laparotomy group. Filtering, normalization, Wald testing, and Benjamini–Hochberg adjustment were performed separately for the two analyses. Because different animals contributed at the two times, these models represented separate cross-sectional contrasts rather than paired or longitudinal comparisons. Detailed filtering and identifier-mapping procedures are provided in the Supplementary Methods.

Shared features from all 32 old-animal libraries were used to compare the treatment-associated contrasts between the deposited 6-h and 48-h experiments. A DESeq2 model included time, treatment, and their interaction. The 6-h time and source-labeled control group were used as the reference levels. The interaction coefficient was defined as `(48-h exposed − 48-h control) − (6-h exposed − 6-h control)`. A positive coefficient therefore indicated that the treatment-associated contrast was more positive in the deposited 48-h experiment than in the deposited 6-h experiment. Genes were ranked by the signed Wald statistic for this interaction. Preranked enrichment analysis was then applied to all 50 mouse MSigDB Hallmark sets, with Benjamini–Hochberg adjustment across the complete collection. Different animals contributed at the two times, and the two count matrices were deposited separately. Sampling time was therefore inseparable from count matrix and from any unrecorded processing or sequencing differences aligned with the two experiments. The interaction was interpreted as a between-experiment difference in treatment-associated contrast, not as a within-animal trajectory or as replication or validation of GSE267933. Complete filtering, mapping, and enrichment settings are provided in the Supplementary Methods.

## Statistical analysis and reproducibility

The animal was the biological unit for all between-group analyses, with one sequencing library per animal. Cells from the same animal were treated as nested observations, and cell-level statistics were descriptive. For animal-level composition, transcript-panel, and module-score summaries, mean differences were defined as the combined anesthesia-plus-laparotomy group minus the oxygen-control group. Uncertainty was summarized using Welch–Satterthwaite 95% confidence intervals, and Cohen’s *d* was reported as a standardized effect-size estimate. For the full three-versus-three comparisons, exact two-sided label-permutation tests evaluated all 20 possible assignments of three mice to each group. Each allocation and its group-reversed counterpart produced mean differences of equal magnitude. The smallest possible exact *P* value was therefore 2/20 = 0.1. These permutation results were interpreted descriptively because the source report did not describe the allocation procedure. Systematic one-mouse omissions were used as influence diagnostics and were not treated as independent experiments or hypothesis tests. Exact permutation *P* values were not calculated for the resulting three-versus-two comparisons. A nonsignificant result was not interpreted as evidence of absence or attributed automatically to insufficient power. Between-group estimates were interpreted as associations with the observed exposure contrast. They were not interpreted as causal effects or attributed specifically to anesthesia, laparotomy, their interaction, or aging.

Analyses were performed in R 4.5.2 and Python 3.11. Random seeds were fixed and recorded separately for each stochastic procedure. Complete software and package versions, function settings, and seed assignments are provided in the Supplementary Methods. Machine-readable tables retain the complete differential-expression, enrichment, parameter-sensitivity, marker, provenance, and influence outputs. The accompanying reproducibility package contains analysis and figure-generation scripts, input and output SHA-256 manifests, execution metadata, and session information. Code-repository and source-data links are provided in the Data Availability statement.
